## Task B:  Product Analysis
Product analysis: Staple remover

<img width="3198" height="3046" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8f18ee0d-850a-4fd4-8eb8-7b57da3039ca" />


Description: a staple remover is a small mechanical tool used to dispose staples from paper. it operates by using two handles that behave like levers. when the handles are squeezed together, the pointed jaws move apart from one another and pull the staple out of the paper.

a. Primary function: the engineering function of a staple remover is to convert an applied hand force into a mechanical lifting force that mremoves a staple from a sheet of material. it accomplishes this by using two levers that rotate about a pivot. the jaws engage the underside of the staple, and rotation of the levers causes the jaws to lift the staple upward and out of the paper

b. Governing model:

<img width="3024" height="4032" alt="IMG_2924" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2b3bbee2-737b-4558-b9af-d4bdf769e9e2" />

This shows that the total torque(moment)around the pivot is zero when the staple remover is in equilibrium. For the staple remover, there are two forces: the hand force turning the handle and the staple's resistance which creates an opposing moment. So, for the staple remover to be balanced at that instant: moment from hand = moment from staple which gives us: F hand times D hand = F staple times d staple.

Assumption: One assumption is that the levers and pivot are rigid and friction at the pivot of negligible. This assumption allows the staple remover to be treated as an ideal lever, where the input moment from the user's hand is equal to the output moment acting on the staple. 

c. Components and Geometry:

Component1: Upper lever

<img width="286" height="156" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f65bd08d-18a5-4b58-a95b-8acbd771f0bf" />

The upper level is the long metal member that the user pressers with their fingers. its geometry is important because it creates a relatively large distance between the user's hand and the pivot. Increasing this distance increases the moment produced by the hand. the jaw's tapered geometry allows it to contact the staple and lift it as the lever rotates.

Component2: Lower Lever

<img width="343" height="168" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a9832f1d-2985-4b8c-9f49-1d0ab77d3ce2" />

The lover level work with the upper level to support and extract staple. The pointed geometry of the bottom jaw allows it to get underneath the opposite side of the staple. when the two levers rotate toward each other, the jaws engage the staple and generate upward forces. it also proved a reaction force against the paper, allowing the staple remover to transfer the applied hand force into the staple.

Component3: Pivot Pin

<img width="130" height="102" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/47b98f04-8bec-44b8-8b48-067c0aff1780" />

The pivot pin connects the two levers and provides the axis about which they rotate. Its cylindrical geometry allows rotational motion while keeping the two levers aligned. The location of the pivot is very important because it determines the lever arms. the distance from the pivot to the user's hand is large, while the distance from the pivot to the staple is small. this difference represents mechanical advantage (Dhand/Dstaple).

d. Patent research: a patent that describes a similar two-lever staple remover is Us patent No.4,903,945,"Staple Remover," invented by Yun L. Wang. The patent was filed on March 25, 1988, and issued on February 26, 1990. the patent specifically describes tapered extraction surfaces on the jaws. 

Alternative solutions: One alternative for removing staples is a blade type. This type uses a pointed blade that slides underneath the staple and pulls it out as the tool is moved. Another type are pliers. This type uses two jaws that pivot toward each other like pliers. the jaws grip the staple and pull it from the paper.
